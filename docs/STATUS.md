- **Ayla (second heroine):** the design bible (`outputs/Project_42_Pirate_Island_RPG_Design_Bible.docx`) names Ayla as the project's own next vertical-slice heroine and gives her full canon and seven-skill kit by name. That's written up in `docs/HEROINE_AYLA_DESIGN.md` along with a proposed deterministic rule translation for implementation. She is **not** added as `content/characters/ayla.json` yet: `tools/src/validate.mjs` requires every skill's animation to bind only to event kinds `godot-rust/src/battle.rs` actually emits, and requires target rules with real Godot targeting behavior. Five of Ayla's seven skills need engine concepts that don't exist yet (a `Staggered` status, a band-contest reaction trigger, a non-mutating reveal event, ward battlefield effects, intent cancellation), and the character schema requires an all-seven-or-nothing roster. Authoring content against events Rust never emits would validate but be inert — the same failure mode the project's placeholder-art discipline exists to prevent, applied to mechanics instead of art. Implementing those `battle.rs` mechanics is the next real step; `docs/HEROINE_AYLA_DESIGN.md` is written so that step doesn't need to re-derive her canon.

**Summary:** the native bridge milestone is complete. Content and Rust rules for Betty's vertical slice remain in sync and green. The two next real increments are (1) implementing Ayla's five novel battle mechanics in `battle.rs` so her authored content can validate honestly, and (2) commissioning the two production identity sheets in `content/art/still_image_plan.json` to retire Betty's and the razorbeak's remaining placeholders.
